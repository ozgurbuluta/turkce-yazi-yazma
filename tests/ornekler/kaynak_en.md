Remote work is here to stay, according to a new survey by Gallup. The study, which polled 12,000 employees across the United States in March 2024, found that 58% of workers with remote-capable jobs now work in a hybrid arrangement.

The shift has consequences for cities. Downtown office vacancy in San Francisco reached 36% in the first quarter, the highest on record. Kastle Systems, which tracks badge swipes, reports that office attendance in the ten largest metros is still only 52% of pre-pandemic levels.

Not everyone is happy. Amazon and JPMorgan have ordered employees back five days a week. Jamie Dimon, JPMorgan's chief executive, said in a letter that remote work "does not work for younger people or for those who want to hustle."

Yet the data on productivity is mixed. A Stanford study led by Nicholas Bloom found that hybrid workers were just as productive as their in-office peers and 35% less likely to quit. The study followed 1,600 employees at Trip.com over two years.

The likely outcome is a permanent split: some firms will go fully remote, some fully in-office, and most will settle somewhere in between.
